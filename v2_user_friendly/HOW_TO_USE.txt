PHONE SPECTROMETER - USER-FRIENDLY VERSION (v2)
================================================

What it does
------------
Turns smartphone photos of a rainbow (white light split by a prism or a
diffraction grating and projected onto a paper screen) into absorption
spectra of coloured solutions placed in the light beam:

    A = -log10( I_sample / I_reference )

Requirements: MATLAB R2020b or newer. No toolboxes.

Files
-----
  PhoneSpectrometer.m   point-and-click app (recommended)
  batch_example.m       the same analysis as a script: edit settings, press Run
  uvvis_process.m       baseline-correct and normalise lab UV-Vis spectra
  core/                 the calculation functions (used by all three)
  HOW_TO_USE.txt        this file

Quick start (5 minutes, with the example photos)
------------------------------------------------
  1. In MATLAB, go to the folder v2_user_friendly and type
         PhoneSpectrometer
  2. "Choose photo folder..." -> select  example_data/photos
  3. Click 01_reference_white.jpg in the list -> "Selected = REFERENCE".
     A white box appears on the rainbow (found automatically).
  4. Choose "Auto offset (auto-exposure photos)" in step 5
     (the example photos were taken with automatic exposure).
  5. Select 02_sample1_a.jpg, 04_sample2.jpg, 05_sample3.jpg, 06_sample4.jpg
     and 07_sample5.jpg (Ctrl-click) -> "Measure selected photo(s)".
     The absorbance spectra and a peak table appear.
  6. "Save results..." writes a CSV, two PNG figures and a log of all
     settings (for the Methods section).

Without a laser calibration the x-axis is "position along the rainbow"
(pixels, blue on the left, red on the right). That is enough to see where
each sample absorbs.

The steps in the app
--------------------
1. PHOTOS
   Choose the folder where the photos are. Tick "Watch folder" at an event:
   photos copied in from the phone (cloud sync, USB, Wi-Fi transfer app)
   appear in the list automatically.

2. REFERENCE AND DARK
   Reference = photo with no sample (or a cuvette with solvent only).
   Dark (optional) = photo with the lamp blocked, SAME camera settings.
   The app refuses a "dark" photo that is brighter than the reference
   background (usually room light was on).

3. RAINBOW STRIP
   Found automatically when the reference is set. If the white box is not
   on the rainbow, use "Draw by hand..." and click two corners of a thin
   box along the middle of the rainbow.
   "Follow small phone movements" lines every photo up on its blue end, so
   a bumped phone or screen does not ruin the measurement.
   Vertical rainbows and rainbows with red on the left are handled
   automatically; spectra always run blue -> red.

4. WAVELENGTH AXIS (optional)
   Laser calibration (recommended): shine two or more laser pointers of known
   wavelength (e.g. 405, 532, 650 nm) along the beam path so their spots land
   on the screen, photograph them, select that photo and press
   "Calibrate: selected laser photo". Type the wavelengths. A check figure
   shows which spots were found.
   Choose "Prism" (curved fit, needs 3+ lines) or "Grating" (straight line).
   "Estimate from known peaks..." fits a prism model to the absorption peaks
   of samples whose lambda_max you know. It is NOT independent - report it
   as approximate.

5. MEASURE
   Select sample photos and press "Measure selected photo(s)", or tick
   "Measure new photos automatically" for live use.
   Offset correction: use "No offset correction" for photos taken with fixed
   (manual/Pro) exposure. "Auto offset" subtracts a constant so that the
   most transparent part of each spectrum sits at zero; use it only for
   automatic-exposure photos and mention it in your report.
   The Notes column warns when a photo's exposure time or ISO differs from
   the reference, or when the rainbow is overexposed. Such values are not
   reliable.

6. COMPARE AND SAVE
   "Add lab spectrum (CSV)..." overlays laboratory UV-Vis spectra (shown
   when the axis is in nm). UTF-16 instrument exports are read directly.
   "Save settings..." stores the reference, strip, calibration and options;
   "Load settings..." restores them next time.

Taking good photos (most important part!)
-----------------------------------------
  - Phone in a clamp or on a tripod; tape the paper screen down.
  - Use the camera's Pro / manual mode:
      ISO fixed (100-200), shutter speed fixed, white balance fixed
      (e.g. 5500 K), manual focus on the paper.
    Choose the shutter speed so the brightest part of the REFERENCE rainbow
    is bright but not white.
  - Darken the room. Use paper without optical brighteners if possible.
  - Order: dark (lamp blocked) -> reference -> samples -> reference again
    -> laser spots.
  - Samples: peak absorbance about 0.3-1. Darker samples (A > 1.5) let too
    little light through for a phone camera.
  - Save as JPG (MATLAB cannot read HEIC; on iPhone choose "Most Compatible").
  - Copy ORIGINAL files from the phone; messaging apps resize and recompress.

Optical tip: a narrow slit (two razor blades ~0.5-1 mm apart) where the beam
is focused turns overlapping colour blobs into a sharp band and greatly
improves resolution.

Lab UV-Vis spectra
------------------
    uvvis_process('..\example_data\uvvis')
or  uvvis_process                (choose files in a dialog)
or  uvvis_process(files, 'Range', [400 750], 'Names', {'blue','yellow'})
Crops 380-800 nm, smooths over the instrument lamp lines at 486/656 nm,
subtracts the mean absorbance at 750-800 nm, normalises, plots and saves
UVVis_processed.csv/.png.

Script version
--------------
batch_example.m does everything without the app. Edit the SETTINGS block
(folder, file names, laser photo and wavelengths, offset mode) and press Run.

Troubleshooting
---------------
"No bright rainbow found"       reference too dark or wrong photo selected.
"Strip falls outside photo"     phone moved a lot; draw the strip again or
                                untick "Follow small phone movements".
Negative absorbance             sample photo brighter than reference:
                                different exposure (see Notes) - use fixed
                                exposure or "Auto offset".
Rainbow sideways in MATLAB      cannot happen here: the phone's rotation flag
                                is applied automatically.
Found fewer laser spots         spots too dim or outside the strip; draw a
                                taller strip or photograph the spots brighter.
